The currency market is the world’s largest financial market by trading volume. We show
that even in this highly liquid market exposure to liquidity risk commands an economically
significant risk premium of up to 3.6% per year. Liquidity risk is not subsumed by exist-
ing currency risk factors and successfully prices the cross-section of currency excess returns.
Moreover, we find that liquidity risk and carry trade premia are correlated, although this
correlation is limited to static rather than dynamic carry trades. Building upon this result, we
propose a liquidity-based explanation for the carry trade, which adds significant explanatory
power to existing theories.
