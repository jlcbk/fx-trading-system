We isolate a U.S. dollar currency premium by comparing corporate bonds issued in the 
dollar and the euro by ﬁrms outside the U.S. and euro a rea. W e make several empirical 
observations that dissect the perceived advantage of borrowing in the dollar. First, while 
the dollar dominates global debt issuance, borrowing costs in the dollar are more expensive 
without a currency hedge and about the same with a currency hedge when compared to the 
euro. This observed parity in currency-hedged corporate borrowing stands in contrast to 
the persistent deviation from covered interest parity in risk-free rates. Second, we observe
