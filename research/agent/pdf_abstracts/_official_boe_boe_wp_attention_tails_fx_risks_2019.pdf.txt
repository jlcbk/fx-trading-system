We document how the entire distribution of exchange rate returns responds to changes in global financial 
conditions. We measure global financial conditions as the common component of country-specific 
financial condition indices, computed consistently across a large panel of developed and emerging 
economies. Based on quantile regression results, we provide a characterisation and ranking of the tail 
behaviour of a large sample of currencies in response to a tightening of global financial conditions, 
corroborating some of the prevailing narratives about safe haven and risky currencies. We then carry out a 
portfolio sorting exercise to identify the macroeconomic fundamentals associated with such different tail 
behaviour, and find that currency portfolios sorted on the basis of relative interest rates, current account 
balances and levels of international reserves display a higher likelihood of large losses in response to a 
tightening of global financial conditions.
Key words: Exchange rates, tail risks, financial conditions indices, global financial cycle, quantile regression.
