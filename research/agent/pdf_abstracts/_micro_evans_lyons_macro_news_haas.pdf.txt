title_guess: How Is Macro News Transmitted to Exchange Rates?

This paper tests whether macroeconomic news is transmitted to exchange rates 
via induced transactions, and if so, what share occurs via transactions versus 
traditional direct adjustment of price. We identify the link between order flow 
and macro news using a heteroskedasticity-based approach. This involves jointly 
testing (1) whether macro news flow increases order flow volatility and (2) 
whether the induced order flow has signed (first moment) effects on the exchange 
rate. The answer to both questions is yes: in both daily and intra-daily data, order 
flow is considerably more volatile when macro news is flowing, and these signed 
orders have the theoretically predicted effects on the exchange rate’s direction. Of 
news’ total price effect, induced order flow accounts for two-thirds, with direct 
news effects accounting for one-third. In terms of total exchange rate variation, 
the order flow channel brings news’ explanatory power up to 30 percent, versus 
estimates in the 1–5 percent range from existing literature, helping to resolve the 
puzzle of missing news effects.
