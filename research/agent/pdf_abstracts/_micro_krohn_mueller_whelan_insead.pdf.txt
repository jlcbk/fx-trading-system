This paper documents a new stylised fact in foreign exchange markets: intraday currency
returns display prolonged reversals around the major benchmark ﬁxings, characterised by an
appreciation of the U.S. dollar pre-ﬁxing and a depreciation thereafter. Moreover, they are a
systematic feature of the data being present every day of the week, month of the year, and
during each of the twenty years in our sample. We study a set of risk based explanations
and present evidence consistent with inventory risk models which link intraday dollar order
imbalance to price reversals across time zones.
