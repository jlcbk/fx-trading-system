We analyze the factors underlying the r ecent deviations from covered interest parity. We show that 
these deviations can be explained by tighter post-crisis bank capital regulations that made the provision 
of foreign exchange swaps more costly. Moreover, the recent monetary policy and related interest rate 
divergence between the U nited States and other major foreign countries has led to a surge in demand 
for swapping low interest rate currencies into the U.S. dollar. Given the higher bank balance sheet 
costs resulting from these regulatory changes , the increased demand for U.S. dollars in the swap 
market could not be supplied at a constant price, thereby amplifying violation s of covered interest 
parity. Furthermore, we show that dollar swap line agreements existing between the Federal Reserve 
and foreign central banks mitigate pr essure in the swap market. However, the current conditions that 
govern the provision of dollar funding through foreign central banks are not favorable enough to 
reduce deviations from covered interest parity to zero.
